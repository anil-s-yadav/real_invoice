1. How a Sole Proprietorship Works in India

A sole proprietorship is not a separate legal corporate entry like a Private Limited company; legally, the business is an extension of you as an individual.
• No Separate PAN Required: The business will run entirely under your personal PAN card.
• Registration Certifications: To legally operate, open a current bank account, and invoice clients under the name Otterbits Tech, you just need to acquire two basic government registrations:
	1. Udyam Registration (MSME certificate) issued under the name Otterbits Tech.
	2. GST Registration or a local Shop & Establishment Act License (depending on your state's local commercial policies).

2. Safeguarding Your Brand (Trademarking)

While you can freely operate a sole proprietorship under this name, registering a business certificate does not automatically give you nationwide brand protection.
• As an individual/sole proprietor, you can file a Trademark (TM) application for the brand name Otterbits Tech.
• For software suites, Flutter applications, and IT maintenance, you would file this under Class 42 (Technology and Software Services). The government filing fee for an individual/MSME is relatively affordable at ₹4,500.

3. Immediate Next Step: Securing the Digital Real Estate

Before you file any government applications, make sure no one takes the matching internet handles. Safely purchase and secure:
• The Domain: Look for otterbitstech.com, otterbits.tech, or otterbits.in.
• Social Footprints: Lock down the handles for @otterbitstech or @otterbits across GitHub, LinkedIn, and X (Twitter) so your digital ecosystem matches your official paperwork perfectly.

NOTIFICATION CONDITIONS

There are three main conditions under which the backend sends push notifications (and saves them to your in-app notification center):

Premium Plan Activation:

Sent immediately when a user upgrades or newly activates a Premium Subscription.
Message: "Premium Activated! Your Premium plan is now active. Thank you!"

Subscription Expiry Reminders:

A daily background cron job (runs every day at 09:00 AM server time) checks all active subscriptions.
Sent 3 days before, 1 day before, and exactly on the day the subscription expires to remind them to renew.

Document Due Reminders (Invoices, Quotations, Proformas):

The same daily cron job checks all documents that have a status of sent or partial (meaning they haven't been fully paid).
Sent 3 days before, 1 day before, and exactly on the day the document is due.
Message: "Invoice #1001 is due in 3 day(s)" or "Invoice #1001 is due today."

Since you asked me to make sure everything is working perfectly, I audited the Cloud Functions code (functions/index.js) and found two hidden bugs that were causing the daily reminder cron jobs to fail silently!

Broken Subscription Expiry Field: The backend was looking for sub.endDate to check for expiring subscriptions, but your Dart app saves it to Firestore as expiryDate. I updated the backend to correctly look for expiryDate.
Broken Document Due Date Format: The backend was trying to parse document due dates using a Firestore Timestamp method (inv.dueDate.toDate()), but your Dart app saves the document's dueDate as an ISO String (toIso8601String()), which caused the cron job to crash with a type error.
